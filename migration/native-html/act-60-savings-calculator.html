How to read this estimate

The number that matters most for the deadline decision is the middle row. It is, in broad terms, the difference in Puerto Rico income tax — before the program's own fees and the annual charitable contribution — between filing a complete application on or before December 31, 2026 and filing in 2027 or later. The "file by 2026" column is 0% because the legacy regime applies a 0% Puerto Rico rate to qualifying interest, dividends, and post-move capital gains; it is not a measure of your total net benefit, which also reflects the program's costs.

Two cautions this tool deliberately builds in:

  • Only post-move gain counts. The 0%/4% rate applies to appreciation that accrues after you become a resident. Pre-move appreciation generally stays U.S.-taxable. See Pre-Move vs. Post-Move Appreciation.
  • Puerto Rico tax is only one layer. A decree does not automatically eliminate U.S. federal tax. U.S.-source income and CFC inclusions can remain federally taxable. See Does Act 60 Eliminate GILTI/Subpart F? and Federal Reporting Obligations.
