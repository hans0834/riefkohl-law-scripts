Puerto Rico Asset Protection with Irrevocable Trusts

How Law 219-2012 shields your wealth from creditors, lawsuits, and divorce.

Why Puerto Rico for Asset Protection?

Law 219-2012 provides a modern trust statute with strong creditor protections based on the autonomous estate doctrine, and long trust duration. Puerto Rico’s civil law system means certain doctrines creditors use on the mainland may not apply here. Note: Puerto Rico does not have an explicit self-settled asset protection trust (DAPT) statute; creditor protection depends on proper trust structuring, irrevocability, and independent trusteeship.

How Irrevocable Trusts Protect Assets

Assets you no longer own cannot be seized by your creditors. When you transfer assets into an irrevocable trust, the trustee holds legal title. Three key requirements:

  • Irrevocability — The transfer must be permanent.
  • Independent trustee — Not the settlor or spouse. A professional trustee provides the strongest protection.
  • Completed transfer — Assets must actually be retitled in the trust’s name.

What It Protects Against

  • Tort judgments (car accidents, malpractice)
  • Business liabilities (even if the corporate veil is pierced)
  • Divorce (if established before marriage or proceedings)
  • Professional liability
  • Creditor claims against beneficiaries (spendthrift provisions)

What It Cannot Protect Against

  • Fraudulent transfers — Transfers made to defraud existing creditors can be voided.
  • Federal tax liens — IRS collection powers can reach trust assets in certain cases.
  • Pre-existing obligations — Child support, alimony, and prior court orders.

Timing Is Everything

The most important variable is timing. Establish your trust before any claim arises. Transferring assets after a lawsuit is filed is almost certainly a fraudulent transfer. The lesson: establish your asset protection trust while the sun is shining.
