What Is a Trust in Puerto Rico?

A trust (fideicomiso) is a legal arrangement where a settlor (fideicomitente) transfers assets to a trustee (fiduciario) for the benefit of beneficiaries (fideicomisarios). Law 219-2012 created a modern, flexible trust statute tailored to Puerto Rico’s civil law system.

Types of Trusts

Irrevocable Trusts (Default Under Law 219-2012)

All Puerto Rico trusts are irrevocable by default. The grantor cannot revoke the trust, but may reserve broad modification rights under §3352h—including amending trust terms, substituting trustees, and adding or removing beneficiaries. Provides strong asset protection, estate tax reduction, and multi-generational planning. Must be structured to respect forced heirship rules.

Revocable Trusts (Act 60 IRI Decree Holders Only)

Act 60 Individual Resident Investor decree holders may establish revocable trusts under the Incentives Code (13 LPRA §10854a(b)). Can be modified or revoked at any time. Provides probate avoidance and incapacity planning, but no asset protection. All income is reported on the settlor’s personal tax return.

Multi-Generational Trusts

Designed to last multiple generations (the life of the last surviving named beneficiary, plus 30 years, under Law 219-2012). Assets pass without probate or estate tax at each generation.

Special Purpose Trusts

Charitable trusts, special needs trusts, life insurance trusts (ILITs), and business succession trusts.

Creating a Trust: 5 Steps

  1. Define objectives — Probate avoidance? Asset protection? Estate tax reduction?
  2. Select the trustee — Individual, professional, or co-trustees. Independent trustees are essential for asset protection.
  3. Draft the trust instrument — Must address distribution standards, trustee powers, spendthrift provisions, forced heirship compliance, and governing law.
  4. Fund the trust — Transfer assets by deed, account retitling, or assignment. An unfunded trust provides no protection.
  5. Ongoing administration — Investment management, recordkeeping, tax filing, distributions, and accounting.

Tax Considerations

  • Federal income tax: grantor trusts (taxed to settlor) vs. non-grantor trusts (taxed to trust)
  • Puerto Rico income tax depends on trust situs, residency, and income source
  • Irrevocable trust assets generally removed from federal taxable estate
  • Federal estate tax exemption of $15M per individual (made permanent by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act with inflation indexing)

Common Mistakes

  • Failing to fund the trust after creation
  • Choosing the wrong trustee
  • Ignoring forced heirship
  • Retaining too much control (undermines asset protection)
  • Not updating the trust after life changes
  • Using a mainland trust without Puerto Rico review
